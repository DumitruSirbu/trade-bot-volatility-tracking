# Review of M25 — Paper exploration enablement

## Summary
The M25 plan outlines the activation of strategy v2 (momentum) and the relaxation of specific risk constraints (stress halts, slot limits, and exposure caps) exclusively for the paper trading environment. The goal is to generate a meaningful volume of paper trades for exploration and data collection, without compromising the strict conservatism of the live trading environment.

## Strengths
1. **Strict Environment Gating:** The most critical strength of this plan is the absolute requirement that all relaxations are gated behind `EXCHANGE_ENV=paper` and specific opt-in flags. This guarantees that live trading defaults remain frozen and safe.
2. **Clear Sequencing:** Acknowledging that M24 (open-fill wiring) is a hard prerequisite ensures that the volume generated by M25 will actually result in filled positions rather than just a higher volume of missed fills.
3. **Targeted Relaxation:** By explicitly exempting the breadth leg (and its M23 auto-resume logic) from the `PAPER_RELAX_MARKET_STRESS` relaxation, the plan maintains a baseline level of market regime awareness even in paper mode.
4. **Operational Safety:** The plan mandates a `pg_dump` before the engine restart and before the manual `clearHaltForDate` operation, adhering strictly to the project's DB safety invariants.

## Potential Risks & Recommendations
1. **Configuration Validation:**
   - *Risk:* The introduction of new environment variables (`PAPER_RELAX_MARKET_STRESS`, `PAPER_MAX_IDIOSYNCRATIC_SLOTS`) could lead to runtime errors or unintended behavior if misconfigured or if types are inferred incorrectly (e.g., parsing a string `"false"` as a truthy boolean).
   - *Recommendation:* Ensure these new variables are strictly validated at boot time (e.g., using `Joi` or `class-validator` via `@nestjs/config`). They must default to safe values (e.g., `false` and the standard `2` slots) if omitted, even in the paper environment.
2. **Breadth Leg Isolation:**
   - *Risk:* The implementation in `StressHaltEvaluator` might accidentally bypass the breadth leg evaluation if the relaxation logic is applied too broadly (e.g., an early return).
   - *Recommendation:* The QA wave must explicitly test that when `PAPER_RELAX_MARKET_STRESS` is active, a simulated breadth extreme *still* triggers a halt, while a simulated BTC shock does not.
3. **Mid-Flight Slot Changes:**
   - *Risk:* If `PAPER_MAX_IDIOSYNCRATIC_SLOTS` is reduced in the future while the paper engine holds more positions than the new limit, the `SlotManager` might enter an unexpected state.
   - *Recommendation:* Ensure `SlotManager` gracefully handles cases where current open positions exceed the configured maximum (e.g., by preventing new opens but allowing existing ones to close normally, which is standard behavior but worth verifying).
4. **Immediate Re-Halt Post-Clear:**
   - *Risk:* Step 3 of the post-deploy process involves manually clearing a stale UTC halt. If the relaxation flags are not wired correctly, the engine might immediately re-evaluate the market and re-assert the halt on the very next tick.
   - *Recommendation:* During the 10-minute live smoke test, explicitly monitor the `risk_state` to ensure the halt does not immediately re-assert itself after being cleared.

## Conclusion
The M25 plan is a well-structured, safe approach to increasing paper trading volume for data collection. The strict isolation of these changes to the paper environment ensures live trading safety. Addressing the configuration validation and breadth-leg isolation during implementation will ensure a smooth rollout. Proceeding with the outlined dispatch waves is highly recommended.
