# Reward:risk (TP:SL) geometry sweep — 20260623-1611

Backtest sweep of the take-profit:stop-loss distance ratio over the same soak window,
holding every other parameter fixed. The stop is re-derived to `TP_distance / ratio`;
the take-profit is unchanged. **Position size is re-derived from the new stop (realistic
risk-based sizing) — a tighter stop yields a larger position.**

Context: the momentum stop is normally the *session VWAP* (a structural level, ~4×ATR),
so realized RR is currently ~0.5 (risk > reward). This sweep replaces that structural stop
with an RR-anchored stop to test whether forcing a higher TP:SL is worth it.

**Baseline sizing/stop mismatch (relevant to reading this):** the live sizer (`PositionSizer`)
sizes risk off `1.5×ATR`, but the actual VWAP stop sits ~4×ATR away — so the baseline strategy
risks ~2.7× its intended per-trade budget on a full stop-out. In this sweep the override aligns
sizing to the *new* stop (size = risk budget ÷ new stop distance), so each swept run risks the
intended budget at its stop — which is itself a correction of the baseline mismatch.

## Caveats (read before concluding)

- **Sizing amplification is the dominant effect.** Net PnL / drawdown across ratios reflect
  BOTH the geometry change and the larger positions a tighter stop produces. Read `realized RR`
  and `win%` for the geometry signal; read `avg notional` / `max DD%` for the risk a tighter
  stop deploys. A "better" net PnL that comes purely from bigger bets is not free edge.
- **This replaces the strategy's VWAP structural stop with an RR-anchored stop** — it is a
  counterfactual on stop *philosophy*, not a parameter tweak, and deliberately breaks
  live↔backtest parity for the swept runs.
- **Not strictly ceteris-paribus on entries:** the 1-position slot cap means a different stop
  changes slot-occupancy and therefore which later triggers fill. Check whether `trades` moves.
- Backtest calibration gaps still apply (BTC index-shock understated, ETH leg dead, modelled
  fills). The *relative* ranking across ratios is the trustworthy signal, not absolute PnL.
- **Hypothesis-generating, not decision-grade** on a single window. Confirm any ranking across
  2–3 disjoint sub-windows before re-tuning stop geometry.

## Findings (analyst read — this run)

**Question:** does forcing a higher TP:SL ratio (your 2:1 proposal) — with positions correctly
risk-sized to the new stop — make the strategy worth trading?

1. **The geometry lever behaves exactly as theory predicts — it just isn't enough.** As the target
   ratio rises 0.5 → 2.0, realized RR climbs **0.76 → 1.23**, avg win grows **1.69 → 3.04**, profit
   factor improves **0.21 → 0.29**, and max drawdown falls **31% → 28%**. So a tighter, RR-anchored
   stop genuinely lifts reward:risk. The lever works mechanically.

2. **But win rate falls in lockstep, and expectancy stays negative at every ratio.** Win% drops
   **22.2% → 19.1%** (tighter stop ⇒ more stop-outs: SL exits 3 → 63). Per-trade expectancy is
   essentially **flat and negative across the whole sweep** (−1.35 / −1.48 / −1.47 / −1.42). The
   higher RR is almost perfectly cancelled by the lower hit rate. At the 2:1 run's realized RR of
   1.23, breakeven needs a **~45% win rate** — the run delivers **19%**. The gap is enormous.

3. **The net-PnL improvement at 2:1 (−296 vs −328) is mostly a confound, not edge.** Trade count
   falls 243 → 209 as the stop tightens (faster exits free the 1-position slot → different entry
   population). Normalised per trade, 2:1 is no better than 0.5 — slightly worse. Do not read the
   net-PnL drop as the ratio "working."

4. **Verdict: the R:R ratio is NOT the lever.** Moving to 2:1 modestly reduces drawdown and tidies
   the reward:risk, but it does not create positive expectancy — the strategy loses at every ratio.
   This confirms your hunch that the real issue is elsewhere. The binding constraint is **win rate /
   signal quality** (≈19–28% vs the ~45% needed), not stop geometry.

5. **The more actionable finding came out of building this, not from the sweep:** the live sizer
   risks off `1.5×ATR` while the actual VWAP stop sits ~4×ATR away, so the live strategy **over-risks
   ~2.7× its intended per-trade budget** on a full stop-out. That sizing/stop mismatch is a concrete
   correctness issue worth fixing regardless of the RR question — and it is separable from it.

**What I would NOT do:** re-tune the stop to 2:1 expecting profitability. **What I would do next:**
(a) fix the sizer/stop mismatch (size off the actual stop, or stop at the sized distance); (b) attack
win rate via entry selectivity (the M43 D1a direction) and TP reachability; (c) re-confirm any of this
across 2–3 disjoint sub-windows before acting — single-window, backtest-caveated.
